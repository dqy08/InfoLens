Orangutans of the peat swamp forest

The orangutan is a great ape that lives in the trees of Borneo and Sumatra. Field researchers follow nests, fruiting figs, and long calls rather than tracks on the ground. This page exists so a browser extension can extract a real article and search it.

Adult males may grow cheek pads and a throat pouch. They travel slowly, shifting weight between lianas and branches. A mother carries her infant for years. Fruit availability shapes the day's range more than any single landmark. Observers write down feeding bouts, rest, and display.

Peat swamp forest floods in the wet season. Walking is hard; boats and boardwalks help. Conservation groups map remaining stands of Shorea and keep corridors between fragments. Illegal logging and fire remain the usual threats. Local teams count nests along transects each quarter.

Captive orangutan rehabilitation returns some animals to the canopy after years of teaching. Success is uneven. Wild orangutan numbers are still falling in several provinces. The useful question for a reader is not a slogan but where a specific population still has fruit trees connected enough to support a season of travel.
